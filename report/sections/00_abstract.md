## Abstract

Differential emission measure (DEM) inversion finds how much coronal plasma lies at each temperature along a line of sight, one optimisation per pixel. Supervised emulators need solver DEMs for every training image. We train on the solver's objective instead: a 409k-parameter MLP maps one pixel's six SDO/AIA intensities to the solver's 54 basis weights, trained on Basis Pursuit's (BP) sparsity and noise-band objective or ElasticNet's; solver DEMs only score it. On the supervised side's 153 test images, the label-free model beats the supervised model on every ElasticNet measure (DEM MSE 0.310 against 0.318, AIA MAE 0.56 against 6.32) and on four of six BP measures (DEM MAE 0.074 against 0.100, W1 0.068 against 0.133). On BP its median pixel error is three times lower (0.036 against 0.112) but DEM MSE 1.9 times higher (1.75 against 0.91): flare cores, one pixel in 10,000, hold 94% of its squared error (supervised: 90%). The original failure there was the loss, not training: at band weight μ = 1 the quadratic exterior penalty prices hot plasma out, rating the solver's DEM 2.4 times worse than one without it. A stiffer band (μ = 100) and longer training restore it (flare-core emission 40% to 93% of the solver's), and penalty-method theory gives the band violation as λ_c tσ_c / (2μ), so μ follows from the response matrix, noise and a tolerance. Bigger is not better: the patch CNN loses to the per-pixel MLP at scale, and larger MLPs can blow up on rare pixels.
