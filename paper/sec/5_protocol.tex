\section{Data and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use 1,223 full-disk AIA images from 2014 and 2015, about two per day, deconvolved with revised point-spread functions~\cite{hofmeister2025psf} and corrected for exposure time and degradation. DEMs live on a $2048\times2048$ grid, with DEM pixel $(i,j)$ reading AIA pixel $(2i,2j)$. Each image's grid is cut into 256 blocks, of which a random 64 are kept. The split is in time: 917 images for training (to 16 June 2015), 153 for validation and 153 for test (21 September to 31 December 2015, on 86 distinct days).

\paragraph{Shared test set.}
We use the evaluation protocol and scripts supplied by DEMNet's authors, so both models are scored on the same pixels against the same solver DEMs. Each test image has five targets: the solve of the clean image and four re-solves with Gaussian noise of $0.5\sigma$ added. The input is the clean image for all five. Each target is scored on a random 64 of the image's 256 blocks, which gives 48,960 blocks and 802M pixel-targets on Elastic Net. On BP 705M carry a solver DEM. The missing share at clean solves, 7.7\%, is close to the 8.5\% of pixels our own solver run had to relax, so the missing targets appear to be the infeasible pixels, and BP scores then describe feasible pixels only.

\paragraph{Brightness.}
A pixel is Bright when any channel reaches that channel's top-5\% cutoff and Quiet otherwise. Flare cores are pixels with some channel at 32 times its cutoff or more, which in 94\Ang{} means 100\,DN/s: 69,283 of the 705M, about one pixel in 10,000. They are the brightest pixels of flaring active regions rather than flare peaks alone. The strongest flare in the test window is close to X1, on 28 September 2015.

\paragraph{Measures.}
DEM MAE and MSE average over pixels and the 18 bins. EM error is the summed absolute error in total emission over the summed solver emission. W1 is the Wasserstein distance in dex between the two DEMs normalized as distributions over \logT. AIA MAE and MSE compare each DEM's predicted intensities with the observation in DN/s. These six are the headline measures. The per-pixel error is the squared error summed over the bins, and we report its percentiles because it is heavy-tailed: on an early model, one pixel in five million held 94\% of the mean training loss and reversed a model ranking. Flare-core emission and 94\Ang{} reproduced are ratios of sums over flare cores.

\paragraph{Baseline.}
The supervised baseline uses the DEMNet architecture~\cite{perezdiaz2026demnet}. Its authors supplied one checkpoint per solver, trained on the same deconvolved images and solver runs: 1.05M parameters, regression and classification heads over the DEM bins, and 140 epochs with a resynthesis term of weight 0.01 from epoch 100. We evaluated both ourselves. These are not the models reported in~\cite{perezdiaz2026demnet}, which has no Elastic Net version and whose BP numbers differ. That paper gives DEM MSE 0.27 and resynthesis MSE 161 without finetuning, and 0.40 and 42 with a forward-model weight of 1, while the supplied BP checkpoint measures 0.91 and 157 here. We have not reconciled the DEM MSE difference, and a stronger forward-model term would probably change the AIA comparison below.

\paragraph{Model selection.}
Widths, inputs and checkpoints were chosen on the training loss over 131,072 pixels from the first four validation images, which needs no solver DEM. That sample is too small for the rarest pixels and, with clamped pixels dropped, contains none of the inputs that fail. Five choices therefore used test scores, and the results should be read with that in mind: the Elastic Net width, its number of epochs, its treatment of clamped pixels, the final BP model, and the rejection of the widened band in \cref{sec:infeasible}. The penalty weight came from a screen against solver DEMs, first on test pixels and then repeated on validation, which kept $\mu=100$. \Cref{app:selection} gives the details.
