## Abstract

Differential emission measure (DEM) inversion finds how much coronal plasma lies at each temperature along a line of sight, one optimisation per pixel. Supervised emulators need solver DEMs for every training image. We train on the solver's objective instead: an MLP maps one pixel's six SDO/AIA intensities to the solver's 54 basis weights, trained on Basis Pursuit's (BP) sparsity and noise-band objective or on ElasticNet's; solver DEMs only score it. The final BP model has 1.5M parameters, the ElasticNet model 409k. On 153 held-out images (86 distinct days) the ElasticNet model beats a supervised emulator on every measure (DEM MSE 0.310 against 0.318). The BP model wins four of six headline measures with a median pixel error 3.6 times lower (0.031 against 0.112); its DEM MSE is 1.2 times higher (1.08 against 0.91), and flare cores, one pixel in 10,000, hold 92% of its squared error. BP's original failure there was the loss, not training: at band weight μ = 1 the quadratic exterior penalty prices hot plasma out. A stiffer band (μ = 100) restores it (flare-core emission 40% to 96.7% of the solver's). Penalty-method theory gives the band violation as λ_c tσ_c/(2μ), so μ follows from the response, the noise and a tolerance. Larger models blow up on scores only when trained without deconvolution-clamp pixels; with them a 1.5M model is the best BP model. Small models lose from those pixels (BP 409k 2.37 to 2.79, ElasticNet 409k 0.310 to 0.439), so the ElasticNet final excludes them.
