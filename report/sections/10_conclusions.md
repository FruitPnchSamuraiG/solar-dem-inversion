## 10. Conclusions

We set out to replace a per-pixel DEM solver with a network that never sees its output. Five conclusions follow.

**Label-free training is enough.** A per-pixel MLP that reads one pixel's six AIA intensities and is trained only on the solver's objective matches or beats a supervised emulator trained on the solver's DEMs. The 409k ElasticNet model is ahead on all six headline measures. The 1.5M BP model is ahead on four of six, loses DEM MSE (1.08 against 0.91) and AIA MAE (3.54 against 2.95), and on the typical pixel is 3.6 times closer to the solver (median pixel error 0.031 against 0.112). Without flare cores its DEM MSE is below the supervised model's (0.087 against 0.091). Solver DEMs were needed only to score it, and when the ElasticNet settings changed it needed retraining, not new labels.

**Check the loss before the training.** BP's one failure, flare cores, was in the loss. The network sat close to its loss's per-pixel optimum, and with the band weight at μ = 1 that optimum leaves the hot plasma out, because only 94 and 131 Å ask for it, at under 0.4 DN/s per unit. No training change on that loss could fix it. A stiffer band (μ = 100, chosen on validation) did: flare-core emission rose from 40% of the solver's to 93% with the 409k stiff-band model and 96.7% with the final model, and flare-core 94 Å from 23% to 88% (the solver 92%). Penalty-method theory gives the band violation as λ_c tσ_c/(2μ), and on validation the measured violation matches it pixel by pixel (median ratio 1.00), so μ follows from the response, the noise and a chosen tolerance rather than from a search.

**Relaxing the band works where it should, and μ = 100 stays.** The theory does not cover the 9% of pixels where no DEM fits the band. The solver relaxes its band there, and those pixels carry the stiff band's cost on faint pixels. Doing the same in the loss turns μ, for the best answer, from a trade-off into a convergence parameter: the best answer approaches the solver monotonically as μ grows. Training confirms this at the relaxed pixels: a 409k run at μ = 300 on the relaxed band has validation DEM MAE 0.15 there against 0.50 on the fixed band. It does not pay overall: a stiffer loss still costs the faint majority (Quiet MSE 0.050 against 0.032), so μ = 100 stays. The headline BP numbers do not see this choice directly, since the shared test set has no BP answer at pixels where the band is infeasible.

**Larger models blow up on scores only without the clamp pixels.** At scale the per-pixel MLP beat the patch CNN at every percentile of the training loss on both tracks, so spatial context is not needed. Larger MLPs improve the typical pixel a little, and trained with clamp pixels excluded they failed by orders of magnitude on a few rare pixels. Those pixels are inputs training never contained: a lone channel reading tens to thousands of times its neighbours, consistent with a cosmic-ray hit or hot pixel, among channels the deconvolution clamped to zero. The training mask dropped 40% of pixels, not the 5% first estimated, and so did the validation loss, so neither could see them. Keeping those pixels, with only the clamped channels freed from the band, took the 1.5M BP model from DEM MSE 897 to 1.08, the best BP model we have, and the 10M ElasticNet model from 2,062 to 1.82. The small models lose from those pixels (BP 409k 2.37 to 2.79 at 40 epochs, ElasticNet 409k 0.310 to 0.439), so the ElasticNet final stays at 409k without them. The 10M ElasticNet model still trails it, and an ElasticNet 1.5M model with clamp pixels is untested.

**The remaining error is concentrated, and the worst pixels can be flagged.** Flare cores are one pixel in 10,000 and hold 92% of the final BP model's squared error and 90% of the supervised model's. Up to the 99th percentile the final model is ahead of the supervised one: percentiles, not means, describe the typical pixel. What is left at flare cores is height at the very top (0.53 of the solver's peak above 3,200, 0.71 for the supervised model) and 94 Å. The low end of the AIA joint distributions is the clamp: where a channel was zeroed the observation is wrong, and the solver's DEM disagrees with it as much as ours on 131, 193 and 335 Å. With the clamped channels freed that column of pixels grows (about 5.5M, 0.8% of the test set) and barely moves the scores (AIA MSE 148 with it, 149 without). The most extreme single pixels remain, 124 of 33.5M on the viewer's eight disks, nearly all without a solver DEM. A self-check needs neither a solver nor a label: a DEM must reproduce what was observed, so a pixel whose reconstruction overshoots its observation by more than 100 times is flagged.

Section 9 lists what stays open, including the unmeasured inference speed and the three choices set with test evidence: the ElasticNet width and epochs, and the final BP model.
