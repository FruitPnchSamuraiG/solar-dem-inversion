Flare-Core Follow-up

The meeting suggested three ways to fix BP's flare cores: oversample them in training, train longer, and use a much bigger model. A direct check of the BP loss showed the limit is the loss itself, so the plan became: fix the loss, then test every training-side suggestion on both the old and the fixed loss.

First result: on BP, the loss is the limit, not the training

At flare cores, the BP network is already within a few percent of the best value its loss allows. That best answer is the one that under-fits.

  • The BP loss is convex in the 54 basis weights, so every pixel has one best answer. I solved it directly for 12,000 pixels of each split, 6,000 of them flare cores.
  • Under the current loss, the best possible answer at flare cores reproduces 26% of the observed 94 Å and holds 30% of the solver's emission (validation; test 32% and 45%). The network gets 28% and 38%.
  • The solver's own DEM scores 2.4 times as badly under our loss as that best answer. The loss prefers the smaller DEM: its sparsity term grows with emission, and the band penalty is too soft to stop it.
  • So oversampling, longer training and a bigger model can only close the network's last few percent. On validation the network's flare-core error already equals the best possible; on test it is 20% above it.

Why the loss leaves the hot plasma out

At a flare core, the hot plasma (about 8 MK, logT 6.9) shows up almost only in 94 Å, and 94 Å responds to it weakly: 0.27 DN/s per 1026 cm−5, against 95 for 171 Å at 1 MK. A typical flare core's 94 Å signal (164 DN/s) needs an emission measure of about 600 units to explain. The sparsity term charges about 600 for that; leaving the hot plasma out costs only about 85 in band penalty, because the 94 Å noise there is 11.5 DN/s. So the loss's best answer drops it. BP's hard band does not allow that.

The noise follows photon counting at every brightness (94 Å noise over signal falls from 1.1 at 0.3–1× to 0.07 at flare cores), so this is not a calibration floor. The failure grows with brightness because brighter pixels get more of their 94 Å from hot plasma. A one-channel version of the trade-off gives 0%, 61% and 87% of 94 Å explained at band stiffness 1, 10 and 100; the full per-pixel optimum gives 26%, 81% and 93%.

  • DEM MSE drops from 4.3 to 2.4, but the supervised model's 0.91 is still ahead: its error is set by flare cores, where the stiff networks reach 85–89% of the solver's emission but only 61–68% of the 94 Å.
  • Oversampling on today's loss makes flare cores worse (emission 35% → 24%, 94 Å 15% → 9%), as the loss check predicts.
  • Under their own stiff losses the networks sit far from the best answer at flare cores (9× its loss value for band ×100). Oversampling does not close that gap: on band ×100 it slightly worsens the DEM error and flare-core 94 Å, and only improves the AIA fit. The 120-epoch, 10M and no-clip runs test the rest.

ElasticNet is different: there the training does have room

The ElasticNet network sits 6% to 18% above its loss's best value on Bright pixels and about 16% above it at flare cores. That is a real training gap, so oversampling, longer training or a bigger model could help there. The check can't compare against the ElasticNet solver: our staged ElasticNet labels come from the old α = 1 setting.

Predictions, written before the results

Meeting suggestions on BP

Oversampling, longer training and the 10M model should barely move flare cores: at most the network's remaining gap to its loss's optimum, between 0 and 20% of the flare-core error.

Stiffer loss on BP

If the network follows the stiffer loss as closely as it follows today's, flare cores should reach about 90% of the observed 94 Å, and BP's DEM error should fall several-fold, likely below the supervised model's.

Also noticed

The gradient clip (norm 1) triggers on every training step: mean gradient norm is about 75 on BP and 11 on ElasticNet. The earlier runs had the same setting, so it is not new. It evens out the size of each batch's update but does not down-weight flare cores within a batch. With the oversampled run moving away from its loss's optimum at flare cores, it is worth testing, so a band ×100 run without clipping is queued.
