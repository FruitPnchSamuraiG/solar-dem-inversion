## 9. Limitations and open items

**Validation.** The validation loss that chose widths, inputs and checkpoints covers only the first four validation images (256 blocks, 131,072 pixels). With clamp pixels excluded it never contains a pixel with a clamped channel, and it preferred every model larger than 409k, each of which blew up on test (sections 3.5 and 7.1). The clamp-mask runs keep clamp pixels in that sample, but it is still those four images, and their losses are not comparable with the earlier ones. So three choices rest on test evidence: the ElasticNet width, the ElasticNet epochs and the final BP model itself. No 409k clamp run at 120 epochs exists to compare it with. Validating on every pixel of the validation split, clamp pixels included, is the fix.

**Clamp pixels.** Pixels with a clamped channel are 40% of valid pixels, nearly all faint. The final BP model trains on them with the clamped channels freed from the band; the ElasticNet model excludes them, because at 409k they cost more than they give (DEM MSE 0.310 to 0.439, Quiet MSE 0.122 to 0.212). Only the 10M ElasticNet model has been trained with them at a larger size: its DEM MSE falls from 2,062 to 1.82, still behind the 409k model, with AIA MSE 1.5 × 10⁵. An ElasticNet 1.5M model with clamp pixels is untested. A freed channel is not fitted, so the final BP model predicts about 15 times the floored value there: the low cluster of its AIA joint distributions holds about 5.5M pixels, 0.8% of the test set, and barely moves the scores (section 7.2).

**Remaining outliers and the self-check.** Keeping clamp pixels removes the score blow-ups, not the most extreme single pixels. On the eight full disks of the viewer the final BP model has 124 wild pixels of 33.5M, about the same as the 409k stiff-band model's 126, and its largest overshoot is about 20 times higher. Of the 124, 123 have no solver DEM: they are unsolved even at the 5 times band, so no training mask reaches them and no DEM score sees them. The one that has a solver DEM alone holds 97.7% of the final model's error on its disk (section 7.1). Simple input rules do not isolate these pixels. A self-check does, with no solver and no label: flag a pixel whose reconstruction overshoots its observation by more than 100 times. The viewer applies it; the scores in this report do not. It flags pixels without repairing them, so a user still needs to treat those pixels as missing.

**The band's rule.** Our loss keeps the 1.4σ band where the solver relaxed it (8.5% of our test split). Section 7.3 shows that the solver's own rule, with the excess measured in noise units, removes the trade-off for the best answer, so μ has no interior optimum there. The one training run on it (409k, μ = 300, 120 epochs) confirms this at the relaxed pixels (validation DEM MAE 0.15 against 0.50 on the fixed band) and improves flare cores, but Quiet MSE rises to 0.050 against 0.032, and one validation pass spiked to 4 × 10¹⁶, so μ = 100 stays. A 1.5M model on the relaxed band is untested. The per-pixel weight and the L1 exact penalty of section 3.3 are untested in training.

**The evaluation protocol.** The shared test set's BP targets exclude the pixels where the band is infeasible (7.7% of clean solves, 12% to 13% of re-solves), so the BP headline numbers say nothing about them. The four re-solves add Gaussian noise of 0.5σ, not σ (the generation command uses the default `--noisescale 0.5`). Our network's basis truncates each Gaussian below 4% of its peak while the solver's runs keep the tails (`--notrunc`); the difference is at most 2.1% of an element's emission and moves the price bounds by less than 1%. The ElasticNet solver floors its residual scale at 0.5 DN/s and our loss does not; 1.4σ falls below 0.5 DN/s at under 0.1% of pixels.

**Flare cores.** The final BP model still holds 92% of its DEM error in flare cores, where it reproduces 88% of 94 Å against the solver's 92% and its DEM MSE is 1.22 times the supervised model's (section 6.7). On the 409k model a continuation of 120 more epochs improved flare cores; a continuation of the 1.5M model is untested. The objective-gap measurement of section 6 has not been repeated on the final model, so how much of its 94 Å gap is training has not been measured.

**The analysis of the network.** Section 8 analyses the 409k stiff-band model in detail and the final model in summary (section 8.4). The figures of sections 5 and 6 also show the 409k stiff-band model and have not been redrawn.

**Not addressed.** Inference speed against the solver is unmeasured. The uncertainty head, which needs a distribution over DEMs rather than one answer, and the AIA plus XRT models are outside this report.
