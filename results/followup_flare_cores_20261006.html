Flare-Core Follow-up

BP's flare-core failure came from its loss, not from training. A stiffer band in the loss, trained three times longer, halves BP's DEM error and beats the supervised model on six of ten measures. None of the three meeting suggestions helps on the original loss.

All 13 runs done Winner confirmed on validation Decisions for you below

Final, 10 Oct 2026. All numbers are on the full shared test set (same protocol as the deck) unless marked validation.

The answer in four points

The BP loss itself preferred to drop the hot plasma at flare cores. Making its band stiffer fixes most of it.

  • Cause. The network was already within a few percent of the best answer its loss allows. That best answer leaves out the hot (about 8 MK) plasma, because only 94 Å sees it, weakly: explaining it costs about 600 in the sparsity term, leaving it out costs about 85 in band penalty.
  • The meeting's suggestions on the original loss: none helps. Oversampling flare cores and the 10M model make them worse; 120 epochs trims DEM MSE by 8% but leaves flare cores unchanged.
  • The fix. Band penalty ×100 brings flare-core emission from 40% to 89% of the solver's. On that loss, training longer is the one suggestion that helps: 120 epochs takes DEM MSE from 2.37 to 2.00 and flare-core emission to 92%, level with the supervised model.
  • What's left. DEM MSE is still 2.2 times the supervised model's, set by flare cores, where 94 Å reaches 75% (supervised 85%). The network is still about 1.5 times its loss's best value there, so this is now a training gap, and longer training was closing it.

Why the original loss left the hot plasma out

The BP loss is convex in the 54 basis weights, so each pixel has one best answer, which I solved directly for 12,000 pixels per split (6,000 flare cores). The trained network was within a few percent of it everywhere, so training was not the limit. That best answer reproduced only 26% of the observed 94 Å at flare cores and 30% of the solver's emission; the solver's own DEM scored 2.4 times worse under our loss.

The reason is the response matrix. Hot flare plasma (logT 6.9) shows up almost only in 94 Å, which responds to it at 0.27 DN/s per 1026 cm−5, against 95 for 171 Å at 1 MK. A typical flare core's 94 Å signal needs an emission measure of about 600 units: the sparsity term charges about 600, while leaving it out costs about 85 in band penalty. The noise follows photon counting, so this is not a calibration floor; the failure grows with brightness because brighter pixels get more of their 94 Å from hot plasma. A stiffer band raises the price of leaving it out: at ×100 the per-pixel best answer reaches 93% of 94 Å and 100% of the emission.

Decisions for you

1. Adopt the new BP model?

Recommended: make band ×100, 120 epochs the BP final model. It was chosen on validation and wins on the test set too. Adopting it means rerunning the website images and updating the deck's BP numbers (about an hour of compute).

2. Train longer still?

Longer training halved the network's flare-core gap to its loss's best answer (118% → 47% above it). A 240-epoch run (about 9 hours) would show whether that keeps going. Optional.

3. ElasticNet

Recommended: keep the current model. The meeting's suggestions don't improve its headline error.

4. What to tell the meeting

On the original loss, oversampling, longer training and a bigger model don't fix flare cores, because the loss itself prefers to drop the hot plasma. A stiffer band fixes that, and on the stiff band longer training helps. One encoding (√ + Fourier) for both tracks holds up.
