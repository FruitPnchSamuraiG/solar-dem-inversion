## Abstract

Differential emission measure (DEM) inversion finds how much coronal plasma lies at each temperature along a line of sight, one optimisation per pixel. Supervised emulators need solver DEMs for every training image. We train on the solver's objective instead: a 409k-parameter MLP maps one pixel's six SDO/AIA intensities to the solver's 54 basis weights, trained on Basis Pursuit's (BP) sparsity and noise-band objective or on ElasticNet's; solver DEMs only score it. On 153 held-out images it beats a supervised emulator on every ElasticNet measure (DEM MSE 0.310 against 0.318) and on four of six BP measures. On BP its median pixel error is three times lower (0.036 against 0.112) but its DEM MSE 1.9 times higher (1.75 against 0.91): flare cores, one pixel in 10,000, hold 94% of its squared error. The original failure there was the loss, not training: at band weight μ = 1 the quadratic exterior penalty prices hot plasma out. A stiffer band (μ = 100) and longer training restore it (flare-core emission 40% to 93% of the solver's). Penalty-method theory gives the band violation as λ_c tσ_c/(2μ), so μ follows from the response, the noise and a tolerance; validation confirms the law pixel by pixel, and relaxing the band where the solver does removes the stiff band's cost on faint pixels. Larger networks blow up on inputs that training excluded, lone single-channel spikes among channels zeroed by the deconvolution, which also produce the label-free outliers in the AIA joint distributions.
