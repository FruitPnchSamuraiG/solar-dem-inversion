## Abstract

Differential emission measure (DEM) inversion recovers how much coronal plasma sits at each temperature along a line of sight from a few EUV images; the reference methods solve one constrained optimisation per pixel. Supervised emulators replace the solver with a network trained on its outputs, so they need solver DEMs for every training image. We train on the solver's objective instead: a 409k-parameter per-pixel MLP maps the six SDO/AIA intensities to the solver's 54 non-negative basis weights, its loss is Basis Pursuit's sparsity plus noise-band objective or ElasticNet's objective, and solver DEMs only score the result. On 153 held-out days under the supervised side's protocol, the label-free model beats the supervised model on every ElasticNet measure (DEM MSE 0.310 against 0.318, AIA MAE 0.56 against 6.32) and on four of six BP measures (DEM MAE 0.074 against 0.100, W1 0.068 against 0.133), with a median pixel error three times lower on BP (0.036 against 0.112) and a DEM MSE 1.9 times higher (1.75 against 0.91). BP's one failure, flare cores, was the loss and not training: at band weight μ = 1 the quadratic exterior penalty prices hot plasma out of the DEM and rates the solver's own DEM 2.4 times worse than one without it. A stiffer band (μ = 100) restores it (flare-core emission 40% to 93% of the solver's), and penalty-method theory gives the band violation per channel as λ_c tσ_c / (2μ), so μ follows from the response matrix, the noise and a tolerance. Bigger is not better: the patch CNN loses to the per-pixel MLP at scale, and models above 409k parameters blow up on rare pixels. The remaining error is concentrated: flare cores, 1 pixel in 10,000, hold 94% of the adopted model's BP error and 90% of the supervised model's.
